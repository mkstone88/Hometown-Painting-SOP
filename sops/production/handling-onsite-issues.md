# Handling On-Site Issues

**Owner:** CEM (Customer Experience Manager)
**Applies to:** Any issue surfaced during a daily site visit — quality, cleanliness, products, crew behavior, or customer concern.
**Related:** `daily-site-visit.md`, `job-site-visit-checklist.md`, `customer-journey.md` (CEM Authority matrix)

## Purpose

Fix problems at the lowest cost point — the same day, in person, with the
crew leader. A problem the crew leader fixes the same day costs nothing. A
problem the customer discovers the next morning costs trust, and trust is
what the five-star review is built on.

## Procedure

1. **Identify.** During the daily site visit, flag any X on the
   `job-site-visit-checklist.md` or any concern raised by the customer.
2. **Address on the spot.** Walk directly to the crew leader and discuss
   the issue. Do not wait. Do not call the office first. Do not leave and
   follow up later.
3. **Agree on a fix.** Clarify what the crew leader will do, by when, and
   how they'll confirm it back to you.
4. **Verify.** Check the fix before you leave the site or on the next
   daily visit.
5. **Document.** Note the issue, the fix, and the outcome in the project
   record.

## When To Escalate

Use the CEM Decision-Making Authority matrix from the Customer Journey:

| Cost to fix | Action |
|---|---|
| Under $200 | CEM decides on the spot. Document what was done and why. |
| $200–$500 | CEM decides. Text Matt a summary by end of day. |
| Over $500 | Stop. Call Matt. Decide together before promising the customer anything. |
| Legal / review threat | Do not respond until you have spoken with Matt. |

## Patterns

If a specific crew repeatedly produces X's across multiple jobs, document
the pattern and raise it in a review meeting to evaluate whether to
continue the relationship. A single bad day is noise; three is a signal.
