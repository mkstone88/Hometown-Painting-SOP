# Customer Journey

**Owner:** Customer Experience Manager (CEM)

**Applies to:** Every residential project from signed contract through 30-day follow up

**Source:** Internal Operations Document • March 2026 • v1.0

The Complete Post-Sale Experience Guide.

## Intent

The goal of this document is simple: every customer who hires Hometown Painting
should feel like they have a dedicated person whose entire job is making their
project go well. From the moment they sign the contract to the moment the
thank-you note arrives in their mailbox, the experience should be so far above
what they expected from a painting contractor that they can't help but tell
someone about it. This is what takes Hometown from $500K to $5M.

## The Six Phases at a Glance

| # | Phase | Key Actions |
|---|-------|-------------|
| 1 | Post-Signing | Contract signed → project email sent → CEM intro call |
| 2 | Pre-Job | Weekly updates → exact start date → Friday confirmation |
| 3 | Day One Kickoff | CEM + crew leader on site → checklist walkthrough → before photos |
| 4 | Active Job Management | Daily site visits → daily customer update → quality accountability |
| 5 | Closeout | Final inspection → survey → review request → warranty → gift |
| 6 | Post-Job Follow Up | Thank-you note → 30-day call → referral capture → marketing handoff |

## CEM Decision-Making Authority

The CEM is empowered to make decisions that protect the customer experience.
When something goes wrong, the default is always to find a reasonable way to
make the customer happy. The following thresholds define when the CEM acts
independently versus when Matt needs to be in the loop.

| Amount | Authority | Action Required |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Under $200 | CEM decides independently | Handle on the spot. Document what was done and why. |
| $200 – $500 | CEM decides, notifies Matt | Make the call in the moment. Text Matt a summary by end of day. |
| Over $500 | Escalate before committing | Stop. Call Matt. Decide together before promising the customer anything. |
| Legal / review threat | Escalate immediately | Do not respond until you have spoken with Matt. |

## Phase 1 — Post-Signing

**Goal:** Eliminate buyer's remorse before it takes hold. The customer just
handed over a 25% deposit and won't see a single brushstroke for 2–3 weeks.
This phase is about making them feel confident they made the right decision
and that a professional team has taken the wheel.

### Step 1 — Immediate Confirmation (within minutes of signing)

An automated text and email goes out the moment the contract is approved
confirming receipt and letting the customer know to expect a project email
shortly. This is handled by the CRM (Go High Level).

> **Confirmation text**
>
> Hi [Name], this is Hometown Painting — we just received your signed contract
> and we're excited to get started on your project! Keep an eye on your inbox
> for a project email from us with a few quick items we'll need from you.
> Welcome to the Hometown family!

### Step 2 — Project Email (same day as signing)

Send the appropriate project email template (Exterior, Interior, or Stain) to
gather the logistics needed to execute the job. The email captures colors and
sheens, scheduling preferences, tool/material storage location, pet
arrangements, and final walkthrough availability.

The project email should feel warm and organized — not like a form. The
customer should read it and think: *these people have done this before and
they have everything under control.*

### Step 3 — CEM Intro Call (within 24 hours of signing)

Once a CEM is in place, this call is their first touchpoint with every new
customer. Keep it under 5 minutes. The purpose is relationship transfer — the
salesperson is handing off to the person who will own the project experience.

The call covers four things:

- **Introduction** — who the CEM is and their role.
- **Reassurance** — confirm the customer made the right call and the project
  is in good hands.
- **Timeline** — brief overview of what to expect and when.
- **Next steps** — remind them to complete the project email if they haven't
  already.

> **CEM intro call script**
>
> Hi [Name], this is [CEM Name] from Hometown Painting — I'm the Customer
> Experience Manager here and I'll be your main point of contact from here on
> out. Matt handles the sales side and I take over once the project is
> underway to make sure everything goes smoothly for you. I just wanted to
> introduce myself and let you know we're really looking forward to your
> project. You should have received a project email from us — once we get
> those details back from you we can get you locked into the schedule. Do you
> have any questions for me at this point?

## Phase 2 — Pre-Job

**Goal:** Keep the customer warm and informed during the wait. Nobody likes
silence after signing a contract. Consistent, predictable communication
signals professionalism and prevents the anxiety that leads to cancellations
or second-guessing.

### Weekly Scheduling Update

The CEM picks one consistent day each week and sends a brief scheduling update
to every active pre-job customer on that day. Pick a day and own it —
consistency matters more than which day is chosen.

> **Weekly update text**
>
> Hi [Name], just a quick update from Hometown Painting — we're currently
> looking at [timeframe, e.g. "the second half of next week"] for your project
> start. We'll confirm your exact start date by Friday once we finalize the
> schedule. Let me know if you have any questions!

### Exact Start Date Confirmation (typically one week out)

Once the schedule is reliable enough to commit — typically about one week
before the job starts — the CEM confirms the exact start date and proposes
the kickoff meeting time.

> **Start date confirmation text (Friday)**
>
> Hi [Name], great news — we're set to start your project on [Day, Date].
> Could the crew and I meet you at [address] at 8am that morning to do a
> quick walkthrough before we get started? It usually takes about 30–45
> minutes and makes sure we're all on the same page before the first brush
> goes on. Does that work for you?

**Note on timing:** When the job is first sold, give the customer a two-week
window ("we're about 3–4 weeks booked out"). As you get closer, narrow it
("it looks like the second half of next week"). The Friday confirmation is
the final commitment. Never over-promise on timing.

## Phase 3 — Day One Kickoff

**Goal:** Align the customer and crew on every detail before a single drop of
paint is applied. The kickoff meeting prevents misunderstandings, documents
pre-existing conditions, and signals to the customer that this company runs a
tight ship.

### Who Attends

- **CEM** — leads the meeting, owns the customer relationship.
- **Crew Leader** — attends the full walkthrough, receives any last-minute
  instructions.
- **Customer** — both decision-makers present if possible.

### Kickoff Meeting Checklist

Use the Pre-Job Walkthrough Checklist for every kickoff. The following items
must be covered on every job without exception:

- Introduce CEM and crew leader to the customer.
- Review the signed agreement — confirm scope, colors, sheens, and paint
  products.
- Walk the job site together with the crew leader.
- Confirm colors and sheens one final time before any work begins — this is
  the last opportunity to catch mistakes.
- Document pre-existing conditions — overspray from previous painters,
  existing damage, areas of concern. Have the customer acknowledge these in
  writing.
- Confirm working hours — note customer's preferred crew hours.
- Confirm material and equipment storage location.
- Confirm payment terms — remind customer that final payment is due after the
  walkthrough.
- Confirm communication preferences — text, call, or email.
- Take before photos (CEM).
- Place yard sign.
- Distribute door hangers to neighboring homes.

### Before Leaving the Site

Before the CEM leaves, confirm the crew has everything they need: paint
delivered and correct, tools on site, scope clear, any special instructions
from the customer communicated directly to the crew leader. Do not leave
until the crew is set up and ready to work.

> **System gap — photo storage.** CEM takes before photos; crew leader
> documents any pre-existing damage. A shared system for storing and
> organizing job photos by customer needs to be established. Placeholder:
> photos texted to office until system is built.

## Phase 4 — Active Job Management

**Goal:** Make the customer feel like someone is paying attention every single
day — and make sure the crew is held to the standard the customer was
promised. The daily rhythm of visits and updates is what separates Hometown
from every other painting company in OKC.

### Daily Site Visit

The CEM visits every active job site at least once per day. On simple,
low-stakes projects with a trusted crew, this standard may be adjusted with
judgment. Daily visits are the default — not the exception.

Given that most Hometown projects run 2–3 days total, daily visits typically
mean the Day One Kickoff plus one mid-job check-in. Use the Job Site Visit
Checklist on every visit.

The visit has three objectives:

- **Quality** — prep, paint application, cleanliness, paint lines, correct
  products being used.
- **People** — is the customer happy? Are the painters happy? Does anyone
  need anything?
- **Branding** — yard sign up, trucks parked appropriately, no other company
  branding visible, door hangers distributed.

If the customer is home, say hello and check in briefly. If they're not home,
that's what the daily update text is for.

### Handling Issues On Site

If the Job Site Visit Checklist reveals problems — quality issues, cleanliness
failures, wrong products — address them directly with the crew leader on the
spot. Do not wait, do not call the office first, do not leave and follow up
later. Fix it now.

If a crew consistently produces X's on the checklist across multiple jobs,
document the pattern and bring it to a review meeting to evaluate whether to
continue the relationship.

> Never let a quality issue sit overnight. A problem the crew leader fixes the
> same day costs nothing. A problem the customer discovers the next morning
> costs trust — and trust is what the five-star review is built on.

### Daily Customer Update

Send the customer a brief text update later each day the crew is on site.
Keep it to three things:

- What the crew accomplished today.
- What they plan to do tomorrow.
- An open question inviting feedback.

> **Daily update text**
>
> Hi [Name]! Quick update from your project — the crew finished [what was
> done today] today and is planning to tackle [what's next] tomorrow.
> Everything looking good from your end?

That last question is the most important part. It gives the customer an easy
opening to surface anything before it becomes a complaint. If they respond
with a concern, address it the same day.

## Phase 5 — Closeout

**Goal:** Send the customer off feeling celebrated, not just finished. The
closeout phase is where the emotional peak of the project happens. Done
right, this is the moment that generates the five-star review, the referral
card passed to a neighbor, and the story they tell at dinner.

### Final Walkthrough — Crew Leader Led

The crew leader conducts the Final Inspection with the customer using the
Final Inspection and Payment Authorization form. The crew leader is present
because they are the person with the ability to fix anything on the spot.
Every item on the form must be initialed by both the crew leader and the
customer.

The crew leader's goal: leave the site with a signed Final Inspection form,
a completed After Project Survey, and zero open punch list items.

The CEM attends the closeout when possible. On smaller or simpler jobs, the
CEM may complete their portion by phone immediately after the crew leader's
walkthrough.

### CEM Closeout — Emotional Closure

After the Final Inspection is complete and the customer has signed off, the
CEM's role is to create the emotional close. This is not administrative — it
is the moment the customer transitions from "client" to "raving fan."

- Congratulate them genuinely on the finished project.
- Deliver the warranty certificate.
- Present the closing gift.
- Make the review request using the QR code.

> **Review request script**
>
> We're so glad you're happy with how everything turned out — it was a
> pleasure working with you. One small favor: if you're willing, a Google
> review means the world to a small local company like ours. It's how
> families like yours find us. Here's a QR code — it takes about 60 seconds
> and goes straight to our Google page. Would you be willing to do that for
> us right now?

Walk them through it on their phone if they're willing. Do not leave without
attempting the review request. If they decline or want to do it later, make
a note and follow up in the 30-day call.

> **System gap — warranty certificate.** Create a branded warranty
> certificate template that can be printed or emailed for each completed
> job. Include job address, date, warranty terms, and Hometown contact
> information.

> **System gap — closing gift.** Target: custom laser-engraved woodworking
> item with Hometown logo, approximately $20–$30 per unit. Source: local
> woodworking contact. Finalize and order before CEM hire.

## Phase 6 — Post-Job Follow Up

**Goal:** Stay in the customer's life just long enough to capture the
referral and the review — then hand them off to the marketing system for the
long term.

### Handwritten Thank-You Note (within 48 hours of job completion)

The CEM writes and mails a handwritten thank-you note within 48 hours of
every completed job. Include two referral cards — business cards with
Hometown's contact information designed to be passed along to friends and
family.

The note should feel personal, not templated. Reference something specific
about their project. Keep it brief — three to four sentences is enough. The
goal is for the customer to feel like a person thought about them, not like
they received a mass mailing.

> **Thank-you note (example)**
>
> Dear [Name], thank you so much for trusting Hometown Painting with your
> home — it was truly a pleasure working with you. The [color/project detail]
> turned out beautifully and we hope you enjoy it for years to come. If
> anyone you know is thinking about a painting project, we'd love the chance
> to take care of them the same way we took care of you. With gratitude,
> Matt, [CEM Name], and the Hometown team.

### 30-Day Follow Up Call

Approximately one month after job completion, the CEM calls the customer for
a brief check-in. This call has three purposes:

- Check in on how they're enjoying the finished project.
- Request a Google review if they didn't leave one at closeout.
- Ask if anyone comes to mind who might need painting work done.

> **30-day call script**
>
> Hi [Name], this is [CEM Name] from Hometown Painting — just calling to
> check in and make sure everything is still looking great about a month out.
> [Pause for response.] That's wonderful to hear. I also wanted to follow up
> — if you haven't had a chance to leave us a Google review yet, it would
> mean so much to us. And if anyone in your neighborhood or circle has been
> thinking about painting, we'd love an introduction — we'd take great care
> of them. Is there anyone who comes to mind?

If the customer mentions a potential referral, get the name and contact
information on the spot and pass it to the sales team for follow-up within
24 hours.

### Marketing System Handoff

After the 30-day call, the CEM's active involvement with the customer is
complete. The customer enters the long-term marketing system:

- Added to the monthly newsletter list.
- Included in semi-annual reactivation campaigns.
- Available for reference requests if they indicated willingness on the After
  Project Survey.

The CEM's job is done. The relationship now lives in the marketing system
until the customer is ready to hire again.
