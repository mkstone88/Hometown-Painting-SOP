# Daily Site Visit

**Owner:** CEM (Customer Experience Manager)
**Applies to:** Every active job, every day the crew is on site.
**Related:** `customer-journey.md` (Phase 4), `job-site-visit-checklist.md`

## Purpose

Make the customer feel like someone is paying attention every single day,
and hold the crew to the standard the customer was promised. Our job sites
are our biggest asset for generating new business — or deterring it.

## Scope

Runs between the Day One Kickoff and the Final Walkthrough. On simple,
low-stakes projects with a trusted crew, the daily frequency may be
adjusted using judgment — daily is the default, not the exception.

Because most Hometown projects run 2–3 days, "daily" in practice usually
means Day One Kickoff plus one mid-job check-in.

## Procedure

1. Visit every active job site at least once per day. Prioritize new
   subcontractors, harder jobs, and larger projects first.
2. Use the `job-site-visit-checklist.md` on every visit. Fill it out
   honestly — checks for what's right, X's for what's not.
3. Address anything that comes up in person with the crew leader before
   leaving.
4. If the customer is home, say hello and check in briefly.
5. Send the Daily Customer Update text later that day
   (`daily-customer-update.md`).

## Three Objectives On Every Visit

1. **Quality.** Prep, paint application, cleanliness, paint lines, and
   correct products being used.
2. **People.** Is the customer happy? Are the painters happy? Does anyone
   need anything?
3. **Branding.** Yard sign up, trucks parked appropriately, no other
   company branding visible, door hangers distributed.

## Handling Issues

See `handling-onsite-issues.md`. The short version: fix it on the spot
with the crew leader. Do not wait, do not call the office first, do not
leave and follow up later.

If a crew repeatedly produces X's on the checklist across multiple jobs,
document the pattern and raise it in a review meeting to evaluate whether
to continue the relationship.
